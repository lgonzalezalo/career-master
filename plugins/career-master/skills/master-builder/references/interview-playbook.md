# Interview playbook

Question banks for the introspective interview. Pick 2–3 per message, adapt wording to the
user's language and situation, and follow up on anything concrete. Never read the list aloud.

## Universal follow-ups (use constantly)

- "What was the situation before you arrived / before you did that?"
- "What exactly did *you* do — and what did others do?"
- "What changed afterwards? How did you (or anyone) notice?"
- "Is there a number, even approximate? Time saved, people, money, volume, frequency, errors?"
- "Who used it or benefited? Did anyone say something about it?"
- "Could you explain this in an interview if they asked for details?"
- "Is there any document, email or presentation that shows it?" (→ `evidence/`)

When no number exists, look for a clear qualitative outcome: adopted by others, still in use,
became the standard, avoided a problem, received a request to repeat it.

## Timeline (all personas)

- "Let's list everything in order, without details: studies, jobs, projects, breaks."
- "Anything I'm missing — part-time jobs, volunteering, freelance work, courses?"
- "Were there changes of title or responsibility inside the same company?"

## Deep-dive per role

Scope: "How big was the team/company? Who did you report to? Who relied on your work?"
Responsibilities: "What did a normal week look like?"
Achievements: "What are you proudest of from that time?" · "What did you improve that nobody
asked you to?" · "What problem did you solve that kept coming back?" · "What would have gone
worse without you?"
Difficulties: "What went wrong? What did you learn?"
Tools and methods: "Which tools, systems or methods did you use daily? Which did you introduce?"
Recognition: "Promotions, awards, good reviews, clients asking for you?"

### Role-type prompts

- People leadership: team size (peak vs usual), hiring, development, promotions, conflicts,
  performance issues, restructuring.
- Operations / service: volumes, SLAs, backlog, incidents, process changes, costs.
- Sales / customer: accounts, retention, renewals, satisfaction, escalations.
- Technical: systems built or maintained, scale, reliability, migrations, cost.
- Admin / support: processes owned, errors reduced, time saved, tools adopted.
- Education / care / public sector: people served, programmes, outcomes, coordination.

## Persona: junior

Goal: surface transferable skills and first achievements without inflating.
- "Tell me about your final project / thesis: what did you decide, build or find?"
- "Group work: what was your role? What happened when something went wrong?"
- "Internships or part-time jobs: what did they trust you with after the first weeks?"
- "Volunteering, sports, associations, events you organised?"
- "Something you learned on your own because you wanted to? How do you know you learned it?"
- "When did someone rely on you for something important?"
Calibrate: "contributed to" vs "was responsible for"; courses count as training, not experience.
Typical under-selling: "it was only a student job". Reframe: responsibility, customers served,
reliability, learning speed.

## Persona: returning

Goal: reconstruct forgotten achievements and connect past experience to today's market.
- Start from anchors: "Where was the office? What was the main system you used? Which project
  took most of your time in that period?"
- "Which problems did people bring to you specifically?"
- "What did you do better than your colleagues?"
- "During the break, did you manage, organise, learn or build anything you'd like to include?"
  (only if the user wants — never required)
- "Which tools you used then have a modern equivalent you'd like to learn or already know?"
  (feeds the skills matrix `equivalent` level)
Calibrate: accept "around" and ranges; mark exact figures as `verify` rather than dropping the
achievement. Watch for confidence dips; acknowledge what the user has achieved.

## Persona: experienced

Goal: sharpen evidence and scope; avoid a long list of responsibilities.
- "What are the 3 results you'd want every recruiter to know?"
- "Where did your scope grow — people, budget, clients, systems?"
- "What would your last manager say you are the go-to person for?"
Calibrate: peak vs usual team sizes, budget owned vs influenced, "led" vs "coordinated".

## Persona: career-change

Goal: extract skills that travel to the target field and evidence of commitment.
- "What parts of your past jobs look most like the job you want?"
- "What have you already done toward the new field — courses, projects, side work?"
- "What do you bring that people inside that field usually lack?"

## Conflicts

Ask one at a time, neutrally: "Your 2017 CV says a team of 10 and your LinkedIn says 6 — which
is right, or were they different periods?" Record the decision in `verification_log`.

## Reflection

Ask in a relaxed tone; write answers in the user's words in `profile/reflection.md`.
- "Looking back, in which job or period did you feel most energised? Why?"
- "Which achievement are you proudest of, even if it doesn't look impressive on paper?"
- "What kind of work drains you or would you prefer not to repeat?"
- "What do people usually come to you for?"
- "Which values matter to you in a company or team?"
- "What conditions do you need to do your best work (autonomy, structure, pace, remote…)?"
- "If the next job goes well, what will you be doing in two years?"
Close with 3–5 "What I am looking for" statements the user approves. These guide which offers
to pursue, not only how to apply.
